## Atmospheric Reality Gap

Your homepage carries 31 content images and zero of them have been submitted for visual review here — so this audit speaks to infrastructure, not aesthetics. What the forensic data does expose is this: 25 of those 31 images (81%) are missing alt text entirely. That means the visual layer you've invested in building is functionally invisible to search engines and to any procurement officer using an accessibility-compliant browser or screen reader. For a company selling precision instrumentation to national labs, universities, and defense contractors — buyers who run rigorous vendor qualification processes — invisible assets signal institutional carelessness before a single spec sheet is opened.

The compounding trust cost: your `og:image` tag is missing. Every time someone shares a Hummingbird Scientific URL on LinkedIn, Slack, or in an email thread — the exact channels where a department head forwards a vendor to a procurement committee — that share renders as a blank card. Your competitor's link previews with a product image. Yours shows nothing. That is not a tie. That is a lost first impression in the venue where B2B purchasing conversations actually start.

## Visual Narrative Coherence

Your link audit returned zero broken links across 25 homepage links — a clean structural record that reflects operational discipline. That discipline makes the metadata gap more conspicuous, not less. A buyer who clicks through from a shared URL that rendered as a blank card, lands on a page whose visual assets are 81% inaccessible to the infrastructure carrying them, is receiving a fractured signal: the engineering is tight, but the presentation layer wasn't built for external scrutiny.

Your navigation architecture spans TEM, SEM, X-Ray, and Custom product lines across seven manufacturer pages and six application pages. That is a deep catalog. When the images populating those branches carry no descriptive metadata — filenames like `e-chem_homepage.jpg` and `hummingbird-electrochemistry-Li-ion-battery-nanoparticles.jpg` appear in the scrape, the latter being a functional filename exception — a high-value buyer comparing you against a competitor whose product imagery is fully indexed and searchable encounters a coherence gap that reads as under-investment, not specialization.

## Metadata and Conversion Gaps

Your page title — "Hummingbird Scientific | In-Situ TEM Specimen Holders" at 53 characters — is serviceable, and you rank #1 for your brand name, which confirms direct-search demand is intact. The problem is category search. Buyers who don't yet know your name — a materials science postdoc at a new institution, a procurement officer sourcing for a cryo-EM expansion — are searching "in-situ TEM holder electrochemistry" or "liquid cell TEM supplier." Your meta description at 139 characters exists but was not surfaced in the scrape as visible in the evidence; regardless, with 81% of your 31 homepage images lacking alt text, you are forfeiting the image-search surface area where instrumentation buyers increasingly begin technical vendor research.

The `og:image` absence is a computable forfeit: every social share, every Slack paste, every email forward of your URL produces a blank preview. In a sales cycle where a $40,000–$150,000 holder system requires committee approval, the number of blind link previews circulating among decision-makers before your first conversation is a direct tax on close rate.

The one immediate, zero-cost correction with measurable impact: alt-text on 25 images and an `og:image` tag. The images exist. They simply aren't working.
